# JSF Brand Agency Engagement Plan

> One integrated agency partner from discovery through activation. Strategy leads; identity,
> copy, experience, and marketing are executions of the same decisions.

## Engagement outcome

The engagement ends only when JSF has:

1. a ratified brand strategy that explains what JSF is, who it serves first, why it matters, and
   what it can credibly promise;
2. a usable relationship between the foundation and every project cell;
3. a verbal and visual identity that works in public, product, governance, and contributor contexts;
4. a complete public story and conversion journey, not only a logo or presentation;
5. an activated 90-day go-to-market system with owners, assets, baselines, and learning loops; and
6. internal rules that allow a future cohort to use and evolve the brand without either founder.

## Operating model

The agency is one accountable body. Research, strategy, copy, creative direction, design,
experience, content, launch, and measurement do not become disconnected vendor handoffs.

The work is still gated. A later phase may be prepared early, but no permanent creative decision is
approved before the strategic decision it expresses.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Agency | Facilitate, research, challenge, synthesize, recommend, create, produce, activate, measure |
| Founding cell | Supply truth and access, resolve constitutional/business choices, approve gates |
| Stakeholders | Supply lived evidence and test clarity; advise but do not become a design committee |
| Legal/institutional/privacy reviewers | Validate claims and risks in their domain; do not direct taste |
| Future brand owner | Operate the system after launch and maintain the evidence/asset repository |

### Decision protocol

- One founder is named the accountable brand approver before strategy begins.
- Both founders answer discovery pre-work independently.
- Feedback is consolidated into one written response per review round.
- Feedback states the violated objective, audience need, factual issue, or agreed criterion. “I do
  not like it” is data about taste, not by itself a strategic reason.
- Constitutional, affiliation, ownership, privacy, and legal decisions remain founder decisions.
- The agency owns recommendations and execution within the approved strategy.
- Unresolved disagreements receive a decision date, decider, and evidence requirement. They do not
  silently pass downstream into design.

## Critical path

```text
Truth and permission
  → audience priority and evidence
    → positioning and brand architecture
      → narrative and verbal identity
        → creative territory and identity system
          → journeys, copy, and asset production
            → internal adoption
              → public activation
                → measurement and optimization
```

The indicative working duration is 12–14 weeks plus a 90-day activation period. The calendar starts
only after the founding cell confirms access, approver, availability, and any fixed launch date.

## Phase 1 — Founder alignment and permission map

**Indicative duration:** week 1

### 1.1 Kickoff logistics

- Name the accountable approver, review participants, response times, and escalation rule.
- Confirm founder availability, budget bands, launch constraints, and production capacity.
- Create the decision log, research repository, contact list, interview consent language, and risk
  register.

**Output:** signed working brief and decision-rights map.

### 1.2 Independent founder pre-work

- Krishna and Satyam answer every starred question independently.
- The agency codes answers as facts, beliefs, tensions, contradictions, constraints, opportunities,
  risks, and decisions.
- The agency prepares a delta report showing where the founders align or diverge.

**Output:** founder-alignment matrix with no false consensus.

### 1.3 Two individual founder interviews

- 90 minutes each, recorded or transcribed with consent.
- Use examples and counterfactuals to move beyond rehearsed mission language.
- Probe personal ambition, founder control, failure, succession, institutional relationship, and
  decisions the other founder may find difficult.

**Output:** interview summaries, quotes for internal evidence, and follow-up requests.

### 1.4 Affiliation, ownership, and permission audit

- Establish JSF’s actual legal/entity status.
- Confirm the relationship with IIM Calcutta and permitted use of name, marks, campus imagery,
  channels, data, and statements of affiliation.
- Record who owns code, domains, identity assets, product data, and future trademarks.
- Mark every existing public claim as safe, needs qualification, needs evidence, or must stop.

**Output:** claims-and-permissions matrix.

### 1.5 Joint founder workshop

- Review disagreements rather than presenting a polished summary.
- Decide what JSF is now, what it may become, and what it must not imply.
- Agree the preliminary primary audience, 12-month behavior objective, and brand architecture options
  to test.

**Gate 1:** founders ratify the factual foundation, permission boundaries, decision rights, and the
research hypotheses. No naming or permanent identity work starts without this gate.

## Phase 2 — Audience, culture, category, and experience research

**Indicative duration:** weeks 2–3

### 2.1 Stakeholder sampling

- Recruit a small, deliberately varied sample across technical students, non-technical students,
  alumni, product users, recruiters, staff/administration, contributors, and skeptics.
- Avoid using only founder friends or early supporters.
- Record relationship, batch/context, current awareness, and potential bias for each participant.

**Output:** research sample and coverage gaps.

### 2.2 Depth interviews

- Ask participants to describe actual recent behavior before asking opinions about JSF.
- Test the problem of abandoned campus projects, trust in student-led systems, contribution motives,
  institutional cues, privacy concerns, and barriers to first action.
- Show the current website only after unaided questions to avoid priming.
- Test comprehension of foundation, cell, open source, governance, and handover language.

**Output:** interview evidence bank, not persona fiction.

### 2.3 Journey observation

- Ask prospective contributors to attempt the current “start contributing” journey.
- Observe where they hesitate: category understanding, GitHub account, issue choice, technical setup,
  role fit, response expectations, or fear of public failure.
- Run a parallel journey for a product user and an institutional stakeholder.

**Output:** current-state journey maps with friction severity and owner.

### 2.4 Category and alternative audit

- Study foundations, open-source governance bodies, campus developer groups, alumni platforms,
  student clubs, institutional labs, and relevant product alternatives.
- Compare promise, proof, audience, architecture, voice, visual codes, CTA, contributor ladder, and
  operational model.
- Include “do nothing,” informal WhatsApp coordination, one-off founder projects, and existing campus
  systems as alternatives.

**Output:** category map and whitespace, not a mood-board collage.

### 2.5 Cultural and semiotic audit

- Catalogue the visual and verbal codes of Joka across batches without relying only on founder
  nostalgia.
- Distinguish owned/permission-safe source material from protected institutional marks.
- Examine how “foundation,” “source,” “cell,” permanence, archives, handover, and open work are
  represented in adjacent categories.

**Output:** culturally authentic source-material library and avoid-list.

### 2.6 Baseline measurement

- Capture current awareness/comprehension from the sample.
- Record web visits, CTA use, contribution starts/completions, active contributors, response times,
  second-contribution rate, product referral paths, and channel performance where data exists.
- Explicitly mark unavailable data and define instrumentation needed before launch.

**Output:** baseline dashboard specification.

**Gate 2:** founders accept the research findings, priority audience, behavior objective, and the
problem definition. Founder preference does not overwrite contrary evidence without a logged choice.

## Phase 3 — Brand strategy

**Indicative duration:** weeks 4–5

### 3.1 Audience strategy

- Rank audiences as primary actor, enabling audience, trust audience, beneficiary, and future
  audience.
- Define each group’s job, barrier, desired belief, credible proof, action, relationship owner, and
  acceptable acquisition cost in effort or money.
- Choose what the brand will not optimize for this year.

**Output:** audience priority and behavior matrix.

### 3.2 Brand core

- Write purpose as the human change JSF exists to make.
- Write mission as the work JSF performs now.
- Write vision as a falsifiable future condition.
- Convert values into required, rewarded, and prohibited behaviors.
- Define the strategic tension JSF is built to resolve.

**Output:** brand core with behavioral tests.

### 3.3 Positioning routes

- Develop three genuinely different routes, each containing audience, frame of reference, problem,
  promise, reason to believe, sacrifice, personality, and implications.
- Pressure-test each route against every project, audience, permission boundary, and future scenario.
- Score routes for relevance, distinctiveness, credibility, stretch, usability, and defensibility.

**Output:** positioning options and recommendation.

### 3.4 Brand architecture

- Compare branded-house, endorsed-brand, and house-of-brands models.
- Decide the visible role of JSF in Joka CV, AlumERP, Joka Connect, content, communities, and future
  cells.
- Establish naming, endorsement, reputation transfer, identity inheritance, data/trust, and retirement
  rules.
- Test how the system behaves when a cell launches, becomes commercial, changes category, fails, or
  retires.

**Output:** brand-architecture map and governance rules.

### 3.5 Proof system

- For every promise, define the mechanism, observable behavior, artifact, current evidence, missing
  evidence, and owner.
- Turn handover, contribution-gated authority, public decisions, charters, privacy boundaries, and
  retirement into visible proof where they are real.
- Remove or qualify claims the operation cannot support yet.

**Output:** claim–proof ledger.

### 3.6 Strategic platform

- Select the positioning.
- Define essence, promise, reasons to believe, character, audience hierarchy, category, strategic
  tension, experience principles, narrative premise, and 12-month objective.
- Add explicit “not this” boundaries to prevent drift.

**Gate 3:** founding cell ratifies one strategic platform and brand architecture in writing.

## Phase 4 — Narrative, messaging, and verbal identity

**Indicative duration:** week 6

### 4.1 Narrative system

- Write the origin, present conflict, mechanism, invitation, future, and succession story.
- Make the community or next cohort the protagonist; place founders in an accurate supporting role.
- Create versions for 10 seconds, 30 seconds, two minutes, one page, and a long-form manifesto.

### 4.2 Messaging architecture

- Build foundation-level master message and audience-specific value propositions.
- Create project endorsement language and cell-specific bridges back to JSF.
- Define message sequence by journey stage: unaware, curious, evaluating, first action, repeat action,
  leader, institutional partner.

### 4.3 Verbal identity

- Choose voice principles that describe observable writing behavior.
- Define tone shifts for social, documentation, governance, product, recruitment, fundraising,
  incidents, and retirement.
- Create vocabulary, plain-language translations, grammar/capitalization, naming syntax, CTA patterns,
  banned phrases, and examples before/after.

### 4.4 Name and line validation

- Decide public/full-name usage and whether “JSF” is safe/useful shorthand.
- Conduct search, domain, social-handle, language, category-confusion, and trademark screening before
  recommending any name change or permanent line.
- Test candidate taglines for comprehension, emotional response, distinctiveness, credibility, and
  future stretch.

**Outputs:** narrative, messaging framework, verbal identity guide, elevator explanations, tagline
recommendation, and copy test scripts.

**Gate 4:** approve language by reading it in real contexts, not as isolated words on slides.

## Phase 5 — Creative strategy and visual identity

**Indicative duration:** weeks 7–9

### 5.1 Creative brief

- Translate the strategic platform into a single creative problem.
- Define required feeling, recognition cues, audience, contexts, functional constraints, inherited
  equity, prohibited territory, and success criteria.

### 5.2 Three strategic territories

- Create three distinct worlds, each with concept, visual logic, typography, color behavior, image
  language, motion, layout, material references, and application examples.
- Explain why every choice expresses strategy and what trade-off it makes.
- Test each territory on the website, GitHub, cell endorsement, contributor call, governance document,
  social post, event poster, and handover artifact.

### 5.3 Territory testing and selection

- Test comprehension and fit with a small audience panel.
- Check accessibility, print/digital production, cultural meaning, institutional confusion, and
  distinctiveness.
- Select and refine one route; do not average the three into a compromise.

### 5.4 Identity system

- Build mark/wordmark only if the selected concept requires them.
- Create typography, color, grid, composition, image/illustration, icons, data/diagram, motion,
  endorsement, cell differentiation, co-branding, accessibility, and misuse rules.
- Produce responsive and small-size variants, monochrome versions, file formats, templates, and
  design tokens.

### 5.5 Recognition and legal checks

- Check visual similarity, trademark exposure, permitted institutional references, font licensing,
  contrast, legibility, and asset provenance.

**Outputs:** identity master files, system guide, templates, token files, and project-cell kit.

**Gate 5:** approve the system demonstrated across real use cases and verified constraints.

## Phase 6 — Experience, copy, and conversion system

**Indicative duration:** weeks 9–11

### 6.1 Journey design

- Redesign priority journeys for contributor, product user, alum, institutional stakeholder, partner,
  and future cell lead.
- Assign one primary action per surface and a human/system owner for the response.
- Add non-technical contribution paths if strategy validates them.

### 6.2 Information architecture

- Decide what belongs on foundation website, project websites, GitHub, documentation, social, email,
  and private operational systems.
- Separate “understand JSF,” “trust JSF,” “use a product,” “contribute,” “start a cell,” and “partner”
  paths.

### 6.3 Core copy production

- Write home, about/model, projects/cells, contribute, governance/trust, partner/contact, blog/content,
  FAQs, disclaimers, error/empty states, metadata, and onboarding messages.
- Write a foundation boilerplate, founder bios, project endorsement copy, press description, event
  introduction, and partner language.
- Verify every factual claim against the claim–proof ledger.

### 6.4 Digital design and implementation

- Apply the approved identity to the public website and priority conversion flows.
- Create reusable page/content components and cell endorsement patterns.
- Implement analytics events, consent/privacy requirements, accessibility, performance, responsive
  behavior, metadata, and link integrity.

### 6.5 Contributor experience

- Rewrite the contribution path as a service experience, not only documentation.
- Create role-based entry points, scoped first tasks, time estimates, response expectations,
  mentorship signals, recognition, and next-contribution prompts.

**Outputs:** implemented priority surfaces, approved copy library, journey maps, content model, and
instrumentation.

**Gate 6:** usability/comprehension testing and technical verification pass before launch production.

## Phase 7 — Activation strategy and campaign production

**Indicative duration:** weeks 10–12

### 7.1 Activation objective and idea

- Choose one behavior objective and one organizing campaign idea.
- Define audience sequence, offer, proof, CTA, channel role, cadence, response capacity, and stop
  conditions.
- Tie launch to operational proof—such as a handover, contributor milestone, product release, or
  public cell-health record—not to an arbitrary announcement date.

### 7.2 Channel system

- Assign each channel a job rather than reposting the same content everywhere.
- Use founder voices for lived conviction and JSF channels for public record, invitations, and
  repeatable institutional memory.
- Prioritize channels containing real audience attention; treat new-handle growth as optional.

### 7.3 Content engine

- Convert actual work into four sustainable streams: proof/build record, founder judgement,
  contributor/alumni story, and open invitation.
- Build editorial criteria, consent rules, asset templates, briefs, approval flow, publishing
  checklist, response protocol, and repurposing rules.
- Prepare a 90-day calendar from real milestones; leave space for learning and unplanned proof.

### 7.4 Launch asset production

- Produce launch narrative, website package, founder posts, partner/stakeholder email, contributor
  invitation, project-cell posts, FAQ, presentation, event/demo script, social templates, and press
  kit if media outreach is justified.
- Prepare responses to predictable questions about affiliation, legal status, ownership, privacy,
  product maturity, contributor reward, and founder succession.

### 7.5 Seeding and readiness

- Brief internal owners and likely advocates before public launch.
- Verify every link, issue, form, inbox, repository, onboarding step, response owner, and analytics
  event.
- Confirm capacity to welcome people; do not manufacture demand into a broken intake path.

**Gate 7:** launch-readiness review passes content, technical, legal/permission, operational,
measurement, and incident-response checks.

## Phase 8 — Internal launch, public launch, and live operation

**Indicative duration:** weeks 12–14

### 8.1 Internal launch

- Train founders and operators on strategy, voice, architecture, templates, asset locations,
  approvals, and escalation.
- Run scenario exercises: new cell, partnership, privacy concern, project retirement, founder quote,
  co-branding request, and external criticism.
- Gather questions that reveal unclear rules and repair the guide before public launch.

### 8.2 Soft launch

- Release to a bounded group across priority audiences.
- Observe unaided comprehension and actual task completion.
- Repair high-severity copy, trust, accessibility, or journey failures.

### 8.3 Public launch

- Publish owned surfaces first, then founder and partner distribution, then broader outreach.
- Monitor questions, sentiment, CTA conversion, intake capacity, and factual misunderstanding.
- Respond through one accountable operator and log recurring language from the audience.

### 8.4 First 72 hours

- Fix broken paths immediately.
- Triage misunderstanding into copy issue, offer issue, operational issue, or strategic issue.
- Thank and route new contributors quickly.
- Avoid changing the strategy in response to one loud reaction; escalate actual trust risks.

**Outputs:** live launch, issue log, first performance readout, and prioritized repairs.

## Phase 9 — 90-day activation and optimization

**Indicative duration:** days 1–90 after launch

### Weekly

- Review channel reach only alongside qualified actions and response capacity.
- Review new-contributor starts, completion, response time, and repeat participation.
- Capture exact audience language, objections, referrals, and confusion.
- Publish one piece of proof from real work.
- Repair the highest-friction journey point.

### Monthly

- Compare performance with baseline and 30/60/90-day targets.
- Interview a small number of new and lost participants.
- Review claim–proof gaps, project-cell consistency, internal adoption, and content sustainability.
- Decide what to stop, continue, or test next.

### Day 90

- Evaluate whether awareness translated into the desired behavior.
- Revisit positioning only if evidence disconfirms it; otherwise improve execution and operational
  proof.
- Deliver the next two-quarter activation roadmap and capability gaps.

**Outputs:** 30/60/90-day reports, experiment log, optimized journeys, and next-quarter plan.

## Phase 10 — Handover and continuity test

The brand must pass the same test as the foundation: can someone who did not create it inherit it?

### Handover actions

- Consolidate strategy, architecture, messages, identity, templates, source files, licenses,
  passwords/owners by location only, content calendar, measurement definitions, and decision history.
- Assign named owners and review cadences.
- Train a non-founder operator to create one campaign, onboard one cell, and resolve one governance
  scenario using the system without agency help.
- Record known exceptions and unresolved choices.
- Establish annual brand-health and project-cell compliance reviews.

### Final acceptance tests

- A new student can explain JSF accurately after thirty seconds on the website.
- A non-technical person can find a meaningful first action.
- A project lead can apply the endorsement and identity rules without founder interpretation.
- A public claim can be traced to current proof and an owner.
- A staff or alumni stakeholder cannot reasonably mistake the initiative’s affiliation status.
- A future cohort can locate, use, and evolve the system with a recorded decision process.
- Brand activity can be connected to a chosen behavior metric rather than impressions alone.

## Scope boundaries that protect the strategy

- Branding cannot manufacture official IIM Calcutta affiliation or permissions that do not exist.
- Copy cannot convert a draft constitution, proposed domain, future product, or founder belief into a
  current fact.
- Marketing cannot compensate for a broken contributor response loop, absent product value, unclear
  privacy practice, or founder-only governance.
- A high-performing visual direction will not be selected if it misstates category, affiliation, or
  audience.
- Stakeholder input informs decisions; it does not create a referendum on individual design details.
- Paid reach begins only after the organic journey and response capacity work.
