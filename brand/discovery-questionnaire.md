# JSF Brand Discovery — Founder Questionnaire and Call Script

> Prepared as the working discovery instrument for Joka Source Foundation (JSF).
> This is not a form to complete in one sitting. It is a decision-oriented script for two founder
> interviews, one joint workshop, and selected stakeholder interviews.
>
> Revised 19 July 2026 to incorporate the proposed two-front model: `jokasource.org` remains
> student- and college-community-facing, while the `.com` domain may become a commercial front for
> consulting services, AI proficiency, and project-led proof. The 360Labs reference is treated as a
> business-model and information-architecture stimulus, not a source of claims or identity to copy.

## What this discovery must decide

By the end of discovery, we must be able to make and defend six choices:

1. What JSF is — foundation, movement, community, institutional infrastructure, product umbrella,
   or a deliberate combination with one clearly dominant role.
2. Who it must matter to first — and whose approval, trust, participation, or adoption is secondary.
3. What only JSF can credibly promise, with proof that already exists or can be built.
4. How the foundation and its project cells relate in name, story, identity, experience, and growth.
5. What action the brand must cause in the next 12 months, not merely what it should look like.
6. How the student-facing `.org` and commercial `.com` relate legally, financially, narratively,
   visually, and reputationally without misleading either audience.

## Ground rules for founders

- Krishna and Satyam answer the starred pre-work independently before comparing responses.
- Use examples, incidents, names, artifacts, and numbers. Avoid answers such as “quality,”
  “innovation,” “community,” or “impact” unless you define the behavior they require.
- Disagreement is signal. We record it before resolving it.
- “We do not know yet” is acceptable. “Everyone is our audience” is not.
- A belief is not yet a claim. For every public promise, we will ask what makes it true.
- Built, deployed, piloted, prototyped, researched, and planned are different states. Never collapse
  them into one portfolio label.
- The agency may challenge an answer that conflicts with JSF’s products, governance, conduct, legal
  position, current capacity, or lived stakeholder experience.

## Part I — Independent founder pre-work

Complete these questions separately before the first live session.

### A. The truth underneath the project

**A01 ★** Tell the story of the moment JSF became necessary. Where were you, what had failed or
kept repeating, and what did you decide could no longer be allowed to happen?

**A02 ★** If JSF had never been created, what would IIM Calcutta lose over the next five years?

**A03 ★** What part of JSF is a response to a real observed problem, and what part is an ambitious
belief you have not yet proved?

**A04 ★** Why did this need to be a foundation rather than a club, startup, lab, society, alumni
initiative, software studio, or collection of independent products?

**A05 ★** What personal experience makes each of you unusually committed to this problem?

**A06 ★** What did CRISPR at IIIT Nagpur prove in practice? Give us the strongest handover, the
strongest failure, and the lesson JSF must not copy blindly.

**A07 ★** What are you building for the community that the community has explicitly asked for?
What are you building because you believe it will matter before they know to ask?

**A08 ★** Which part of the current JSF story makes you proud? Which part feels inflated, borrowed,
premature, or difficult to say out loud?

**A09** What would make JSF an embarrassing failure even if its products attracted users?

**A10** What would make JSF a profound success even if it never became large?

### B. Founder intent and succession

**B01 ★** What do you want JSF to become after both founders graduate in 2027?

**B02 ★** What power, credit, access, identity, or control are you genuinely willing to give away so
that the foundation can outlive you?

**B03 ★** What are you not willing to give away, and why?

**B04** If JSF succeeds, what do you personally hope people say you did? What must they never say
you used JSF to do?

**B05** What happens if one founder disengages, disagrees permanently, joins a conflicting venture,
or becomes reputationally harmful?

**B06** Who could credibly lead JSF without either founder? What is missing before that can happen?

**B07** Which founder is currently the keeper of product, governance, community, narrative,
operations, institutional relations, and fundraising? Where is ownership ambiguous?

**B08** When founder preference conflicts with evidence from users or contributors, what wins?

### C. Definition and boundaries

**C01 ★** Complete one sentence without using “platform,” “ecosystem,” “community,” “innovation,”
or “empower”: “Joka Source Foundation is _____.”

**C02 ★** What is JSF not?

**C03 ★** Is “foundation” a legal description, a governance promise, a cultural aspiration, a brand
signal, or all four? Which of these is true today?

**C04 ★** What does “Joka” mean here: a place, a shared identity, an IIM Calcutta shorthand, a
membership boundary, an emotional memory, or a worldview?

**C05** Is the long-term ambition permanently limited to IIM Calcutta, expandable to other campuses,
or intended to become a replicable model that other institutions can adopt?

**C06** Which activities belong inside JSF? Which must remain independent even when founders or
contributors overlap?

**C07** What test should a proposed project pass before it deserves to become a JSF cell?

**C08** Can a commercially successful product live under JSF? Can JSF charge, fundraise, sponsor,
license commercially, take equity, or create a for-profit subsidiary? What should never happen?

**C09** Is JSF primarily accountable to current students, alumni, IIM Calcutta as an institution,
contributors, project users, funders, or the public? Rank them and explain the hard trade-off.

**C10** What must remain open? What may remain private? What must never be collected or published?

## Part II — Live founder interview I: meaning, people, and permission

### D. Relationship with IIM Calcutta

**D01** What is JSF’s current formal relationship with IIM Calcutta: official, recognized,
permitted, independent, alumni-led, student-led, or presently undefined?

**D02** What exact names, marks, photography, campus references, email lists, groups, data, rooms,
and staff relationships may JSF use? Which require permission?

**D03** The current materials variously say “for IIM Calcutta,” “alumni-rooted, not
institution-rooted,” and “belongs to the institution.” Which is factually and strategically right?

**D04** Should a reasonable visitor ever mistake JSF for an official IIM Calcutta body? If not, what
disclosure must appear and where?

**D05** Does “belongs to the institution” mean legal ownership, stewardship for the community,
public-benefit intent, or rhetoric? Who actually owns the code, data, domains, and trademarks?

**D06** What institutional endorsement would materially change JSF’s prospects? What independence
would be lost in exchange?

**D07** What could make the administration, alumni office, placement ecosystem, or faculty distrust
JSF even if the products are useful?

**D08** Where should JSF cooperate with existing campus bodies? Where should it deliberately remain
independent?

### E. Audience priority and human truth

For each audience, we will capture: current behavior, functional need, emotional need, social risk,
objection, desired belief, proof, action, channel, and relationship owner.

**E01 ★** If JSF could earn only one group’s deep trust in the next 12 months, which group would
create the greatest compounding effect?

**E02** Current students: what are they trying to get done, and why would contributing to a
foundation compete badly with placements, clubs, academics, or their own projects?

**E03** Technical students: what makes a contribution feel meaningful rather than like unpaid work
for two founders?

**E04** Non-technical students: what valuable roles can they hold, and does today’s GitHub-first
on-ramp accidentally tell them JSF is not for them?

**E05** Alumni: what would make them return, contribute expertise, trust data, mentor, fund, or
advocate? What has made them cynical about previous alumni initiatives?

**E06** Recruiters and employers: what promise can JSF make that an ordinary CV, LinkedIn profile,
or placement process cannot? What proof would they require?

**E07** IIM Calcutta staff and administration: what operational burden does JSF remove, and what new
risk does it create?

**E08** Open-source contributors outside IIMC: are they welcome, strategically useful, and eligible
for authority? Where does the constitution currently draw the line?

**E09** Funders, sponsors, and partners: what would they be funding—the foundation, a cell, access to
talent, public infrastructure, or a replicable model? Which motive is acceptable?

**E10** End users of each product: do they need to know JSF exists? If yes, at which moment does the
foundation story create trust rather than confusion?

**E11** Who is the audience we are tempted to impress but should stop designing for?

**E12** Who bears risk if JSF gets the brand wrong but may not have a seat in this discovery?

### F. Reputation, perception, and category

**F01 ★** What do people who know JSF currently think it is? What evidence supports that answer?

**F02** What do you want each priority audience to think, feel, say, and do after encountering JSF?

**F03** What is the dangerous current perception: founder vanity project, unofficial campus app
maker, coding club, startup portfolio, alumni database, placement workaround, or something else?

**F04** What category should JSF enter in people’s minds? Is there an existing category we can own,
or must we teach a new model?

**F05** Which organizations, communities, foundations, student bodies, alumni platforms, open-source
projects, and institutional labs will people compare JSF with?

**F06** Which comparison helps? Which comparison creates the wrong expectation?

**F07** If another IIM launched a similar initiative tomorrow, what could it copy immediately? What
could it not copy because JSF has earned it through history, proof, or community?

**F08** What should JSF be famous for in one year? In five years?

**F09** What criticism would be fair today?

**F10** What criticism would indicate that JSF is making a necessary, principled trade-off?

### G. Purpose, values, and behavior

**G01 ★** If “software that outlives its founders” is the purpose, what measurable conditions prove
that a project has actually outlived them?

**G02** Is continuity the ultimate human benefit or the operating mechanism behind a deeper benefit?
What becomes better in a student’s or alum’s life because continuity exists?

**G03** Which three current principles would you defend when they cost growth, convenience, money,
or founder control?

**G04** For each value, what behavior is required, what behavior is rewarded, and what behavior is
unacceptable?

**G05** When openness conflicts with privacy, safety, speed, or institutional diplomacy, what is the
decision rule?

**G06** When merit-based authority conflicts with inclusion, mentorship, or unequal access to free
time, how will JSF avoid reproducing privilege?

**G07** What does “service to the campus community” require when the loudest users are not the most
vulnerable or important users?

**G08** What ritual, artifact, or public behavior could make handover visible and culturally valued?

**G09** What does graceful retirement feel like to the person who founded a cell? How will the brand
prevent “retired” from feeling like “failed”?

**G10** Which values are table stakes for any credible open-source organization and therefore not
useful as distinctive positioning?

## Part III — Live founder interview II: offer, architecture, language, and identity

### H. Portfolio and brand architecture

**H01 ★** Is JSF the master brand people should seek, or an endorsing trust mark behind products
people seek for their own reasons?

**H02** Should the market see one branded system (“Joka Source CV,” “Joka Source Connect”), endorsed
brands (“Joka CV, a JSF project”), or mostly independent brands connected only in governance?

**H03** What must every cell inherit from JSF: purpose, naming, visual DNA, UX standards, contributor
model, data principles, voice, endorsement, or nothing beyond governance?

**H04** What freedom must every cell retain to fit its category and users?

**H05** When a product fails, how much reputational damage should travel to JSF and other cells?

**H06** When a product succeeds, how much equity should flow back to JSF?

**H07** What does “cell” enable strategically that “project,” “venture,” “product,” or “initiative”
does not? What confusion does it create?

**H08** Should CRISPR be a public-facing metaphor, an internal governance model, or both? Are we
comfortable with scientific/gene-editing associations and the burden of explaining them?

**H09** Which current and anticipated offerings are products, programs, content, infrastructure,
communities, or governance mechanisms? Map each one.

**H10** Does the name “Joka Source Foundation” survive expansion beyond software or beyond IIMC?

### I. Positioning and promise

**I01 ★** What important job does JSF do better than the current alternative of a student building a
project independently and handing over a repository informally?

**I02** What are the real alternatives—not only competitors? Include doing nothing, WhatsApp groups,
spreadsheets, clubs, placement systems, LinkedIn, alumni offices, and one-off student teams.

**I03** What is JSF’s single most ownable advantage today?

**I04** What advantage could it build over the next 24 months that would be difficult to copy?

**I05** What promise can the foundation make across every project cell without overclaiming?

**I06** What is the evidence chain behind that promise: mechanism, behavior, artifact, outcome, and
third-party validation?

**I07** What tension should the positioning resolve for the audience? Examples: autonomy and memory,
student energy and institutional reliability, openness and privacy, ambition and handover.

**I08** What are we willing to be narrower about so that the brand becomes more credible?

**I09** What would make an ideal contributor choose JSF over starting something alone?

**I10** What would make an alum or institutional stakeholder believe JSF will still exist in 2030?

### J. Story and verbal identity

**J01 ★** Tell the JSF origin story as a human story, not an organizational description. Who wanted
what, what stood in the way, what changed, and what remains unresolved?

**J02** Who is the hero of the story: the contributor, the user, the next cohort, the institution,
the project cell, or the founders? Who is the guide?

**J03** What is the enemy? Avoid naming a person or competing organization; name the pattern,
inertia, waste, or belief JSF exists to oppose.

**J04** What is the baton that one cohort hands the next—code, responsibility, memory, trust,
opportunity, identity, or all of them in a deliberate hierarchy?

**J05** Is “Built to outlive us” the enduring line, a launch line, or a useful draft? What emotional
response should it produce?

**J06** Is “Software should survive graduation” too narrow if JSF later holds non-software cells?

**J07** What words feel unmistakably Joka? Which campus references are meaningful across batches
rather than nostalgia available only to insiders?

**J08** Which terms must we define for ordinary people: cell, foundation, open source, governance,
incubating, contributor, committer, public record?

**J09** What phrases should JSF never use because they are hype, institutionally misleading,
exclusionary, technically opaque, or simply not us?

**J10** Should the brand sound like builders, stewards, students, alumni, an institution, a public
foundation, or a distinct combination? Rank the voices.

**J11** How should voice change during recruiting, a product launch, governance debate, privacy
incident, project retirement, fundraising, and institutional negotiation?

**J12** When should a founder speak in first person, and when should JSF speak as “we”?

### K. Name and nomenclature

**K01** What does “Source” mean: source code, origin, source of institutional memory, or source of
future initiatives? Which meaning should be foregrounded?

**K02** Does “JSF” help as shorthand, or create confusion with other established meanings? Where is
the full name mandatory?

**K03** Is “Joka Source” the public name and “Joka Source Foundation” the formal name, or should the
full name lead everywhere?

**K04** What naming rule should govern new cells? Must names start with Joka, describe the job, or
stand independently?

**K05** Which current name is weakest or most likely to age badly: Joka CV, AlumERP, or Joka Connect?

**K06** Which domains and handles are actually controlled, and which are only proposed in documents?

**K07** What trademark, affiliation, or search-discoverability checks must be cleared before the
identity is made permanent?

### L. Visual and experiential identity

**L01 ★** Before showing references, choose where JSF should sit on each spectrum and explain why:
institutional–insurgent; archival–alive; technical–human; local–universal; sober–expressive;
precise–warm; prestigious–accessible; permanent–evolving.

**L02** What should someone feel in the first three seconds of seeing JSF? What should they
understand within thirty seconds?

**L03** What visual conventions of universities, foundations, open source, startups, and student
clubs should we deliberately use or reject?

**L04** Which current provisional choices—warm paper, black ink, coral, acid green, serif display,
system sans—feel true? Which merely feel fashionable?

**L05** What existing campus forms could become authentic source material: noticeboards, stamps,
library cards, print culture, architecture, maps, handover documents, code diffs, batch artifacts?

**L06** Which IIM Calcutta or campus visual properties require permission or should be avoided?

**L07** Should the identity emphasize “Joka,” “Source,” “Foundation,” a cell/lifecycle symbol, a
handover gesture, or no literal metaphor at all?

**L08** How should the identity flex for each project without becoming a generic startup design kit?

**L09** Where must the identity work first: GitHub, website, product endorsement, social posts,
presentations, campus posters, events, documents, merchandise, or signage?

**L10** What accessibility, multilingual, low-bandwidth, print, dark-mode, or production constraints
must be designed in from the start?

**L11** What visual territory would feel dishonest, elitist, childish, corporate, official, biotech,
or overdesigned?

**L12** Name three brands or cultural artifacts you admire, the precise quality you admire in each,
and the element we must not imitate.

## Part V — The `.com` commercial front and project-led credibility

This part was added after the founders proposed keeping `jokasource.org` focused on students and the
college community while using the `.com` domain to demonstrate consulting capability, AI
proficiency, and completed or planned projects. It must be answered before the two-front model is
expressed publicly.

### R. Two-front brand, entity, and domain architecture

**R01 ★** Complete two separate sentences: “On `.org`, Joka Source helps _____ to _____ by _____.”
“On `.com`, Joka Source helps _____ to _____ by _____.”

**R02 ★** Is the `.com` offer the commercial arm of the foundation, a founder-owned company, an
independent studio that shares a name, a future subsidiary, or something else? What is legally true
today, and what is merely intended?

**R03 ★** Why should both fronts use “Joka Source”? What trust legitimately transfers between
them, and what trust must be earned separately?

**R04** Which name should appear on proposals, contracts, invoices, bank records, intellectual
property, support obligations, privacy notices, and hiring documents?

**R05** What financial relationship should exist between commercial revenue and the foundation:
none, donation, sponsorship, shared services, licensing, student funding, or another documented
arrangement? Who approves it and how is conflict of interest disclosed?

**R06** What may the commercial front truthfully say about students, IIM Calcutta, alumni, the
foundation, contributors, and project cells without implying endorsement, employment, free student
labour, or institutional ownership?

**R07** Can student or volunteer work ever appear as commercial proof? If yes, what consent, credit,
compensation, IP, safeguarding, and opt-out rules apply?

**R08** When a commercial client, foundation user, or student contributor encounters the other
front, what must they immediately understand about the relationship?

**R09** Which assets are shared across `.org` and `.com`: name, mark, design system, team, project
portfolio, technology, mailing list, analytics, code, office, or channels? Which must remain
strictly separate?

**R10** How should reputation travel between the fronts when one succeeds, fails, is criticized,
suffers a security incident, loses a client, or retires a project?

**R11** What navigation, disclosure, and cross-linking should connect the domains? What should never
be cross-promoted?

**R12** What factual, legal, accounting, trademark, tax, data-protection, and institutional reviews
must be completed before the `.com` accepts money or publishes client claims?

### S. Consulting offer and business model

**S01 ★** Who is the first ideal paying client? Describe company size, industry, buyer role,
triggering event, budget reality, technical maturity, urgency, and the problem they already know
they have.

**S02 ★** What painful business condition are we best equipped to change today—not someday—and how
does the buyer currently measure that pain?

**S03 ★** Which label is most accurate and commercially useful: AI consultancy, AI-native product
studio, software development house, engineering lab, automation partner, fractional product team,
or a deliberate combination with one clear lead?

**S04** When you say “AI proficiency,” do you mean training people, assessing readiness, designing
strategy, automating workflows, building AI products, integrating models, running research, or
operating production systems? Rank the offers we can deliver now.

**S05** What is the smallest valuable paid engagement: advisory call, readiness assessment,
discovery sprint, workflow audit, prototype, proof of concept, production build, or retainer?

**S06** What is the offer ladder from first conversation to repeat engagement? For each stage, name
the deliverable, duration, price logic, decision gate, client responsibility, and success condition.

**S07** What business outcome do clients buy from us: time saved, cost removed, risk reduced,
revenue created, decisions improved, capability transferred, or software shipped? Which can we
already prove?

**S08** What makes us meaningfully different from a freelancer, conventional development agency,
large consultancy, SaaS tool, internal tech team, or a client using AI coding tools itself?

**S09** Which part of the 360Labs model do we want to emulate precisely—speed, senior builders,
portfolio breadth, productized discovery, research credibility, technical depth, ongoing support,
or something else? Which parts do not fit us?

**S10** Could we credibly promise to act “like an in-house tech team”? If so, what availability,
context retention, governance, accountability, and support make that true?

**S11** What minimum fee, project size, gross margin, payment schedule, change-control process, and
capacity limit make the work sustainable?

**S12** What delivery timeline can we repeat under normal conditions? What dependencies or client
delays prevent a public speed promise?

**S13** What happens after launch: warranty, support, monitoring, incident response, model or data
drift review, security updates, hosting, feature work, and handover? Which are included or paid?

**S14** Who sells, scopes, estimates, contracts, builds, reviews, communicates, invoices, supports,

## Agency synthesis rubric

Every material answer is tagged as one of the following:

| Tag | Meaning | Agency action |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Verifiable current truth | Capture source and date |
| Belief | Founder conviction without proof yet | Test in stakeholder research |
| Tension | Two desirable but competing truths | Turn into a strategic choice or creative territory |
| Contradiction | Claims or behaviors that cannot coexist | Escalate to joint decision workshop |
| Constraint | Legal, capacity, budget, technical, or timing boundary | Design within it |
| Opportunity | Unclaimed, valuable possibility | Size and prioritize |
| Risk | Plausible trust, adoption, or operational failure | Assign mitigation and owner |
| Decision | Explicit choice with authority | Log, apply, and create consequences |

The agency does not move into permanent naming, identity, or launch production until the critical
contradictions in affiliation, brand architecture, priority audience, promise, and decision rights
have been resolved in writing.
