# Joka Source Brand Discovery — Condensed 30-Question Founder Questionnaire

> This is the practical breadth-first version of the full discovery bank. Krishna and Satyam should
> answer it independently before comparing answers. Short answers are acceptable, but use concrete
> examples, names, dates, numbers, artifacts, and evidence wherever possible.

## How to answer

- Mark material statements as **fact**, **belief**, **decision**, **constraint**, or **unknown**.
- Distinguish **live**, **deployed**, **pilot**, **prototype**, **research**, **paused**, **retired**,
  and **planned** work.
- Do not use “innovation,” “community,” “impact,” “AI-native,” or “quality” without defining the
  observable behavior or outcome meant.
- Disagreement between founders is useful evidence. Record it before trying to reconcile it.
- If an answer concerns a public claim, name the evidence and permission that make the claim usable.

## Foundation truth, purpose, and continuity

### 1. Why must Joka Source exist?

Tell the human origin story: what happened, who experienced the problem, what kept failing, why the
existing alternatives were insufficient, and why you became personally committed. Separate what
you directly observed from what you currently believe.

### 2. What is Joka Source—and what is it not?

Define it in one plain sentence without “platform,” “ecosystem,” “community,” “innovation,” or
“empower.” Then state whether it is primarily a foundation, stewardship institution, project
umbrella, student movement, open-source organization, product studio, commercial business, or a
deliberate combination with one dominant role. Name the categories it must never be confused with.

### 3. What change should Joka Source make in the world it serves?

Describe the present condition, the future condition, the human benefit, and the operating mechanism
connecting them. Explain whether continuity, institutional memory, student capability, useful
software, employability, or something deeper is the ultimate purpose.

### 4. What does success look like across time?

Define meaningful success at 12 months, at the founders’ graduation in 2027, in five years, and if
the organization remains deliberately small. Also define embarrassing failure even with impressive
traffic, revenue, products, or publicity.

### 5. How will Joka Source genuinely outlive its founders?

Specify which authority, access, credit, ownership, relationships, and decisions the founders will
transfer; who could lead next; how leadership is earned; what handover artifacts and rituals exist;
how projects retire responsibly; and what measurable conditions prove non-founder continuity.

## Permission, governance, and the two-front model

### 6. What is the exact relationship with IIM Calcutta and the wider college community?

State what is official, recognized, permitted, independent, student-led, alumni-rooted, or still
undefined. List which names, marks, images, channels, rooms, data, staff relationships, and claims
may be used, what needs permission, what disclaimer is required, and who actually owns the code,
domains, data, and trademarks.

### 7. Who is Joka Source accountable to, and who has authority?

Rank current students, future cohorts, alumni, contributors, product users, clients, the institution,
funders, and the public. Explain how decisions are made, what contributors can earn authority over,
which decisions require founder unanimity, and what happens during founder conflict, departure, or
misconduct.

### 8. What distinct jobs should `.org` and `.com` perform?

Complete both sentences: “On `.org`, Joka Source helps _____ to _____ by _____.” “On `.com`, Joka
Source helps _____ to _____ by _____.” For each domain, name the primary audience, promise, proof,
desired action, content, and explicit exclusions.

### 9. What legally and economically connects `.org` and `.com`?

Decide whether the commercial operation is part of the foundation, a founder-owned company, a
subsidiary, or an independent studio sharing a name. Identify the contracting and invoicing entity,
IP ownership, revenue flow, student compensation, conflict-of-interest rules, shared resources, tax
and accounting questions, and the truthful public disclosure of that relationship.

### 10. What trust may—and may not—travel between the two fronts?

Define which name, identity, team, work, technology, portfolio, channels, data, and reputation may
be shared. Explain how student or volunteer work may be credited or commercialized, what requires
consent, and how a commercial failure, security incident, client dispute, or foundation controversy
should affect the other front.

## People, needs, offers, and differentiation

### 11. Who must trust the `.org` first?

Choose one priority group for the next 12 months. Describe its functional need, emotional need,
existing behavior, alternatives, objection, social risk, desired belief, required proof, first
action, repeat action, and the compounding effect of earning its trust.

### 12. Who is the first ideal `.com` client?

Describe the organization size, industry, geography, buyer and internal champion, technical
maturity, triggering event, painful current condition, urgency, buying process, realistic budget,
risk tolerance, and reasons it would choose—or reject—Joka Source.

### 13. What exactly are we selling?

Choose the clearest lead category: AI consultancy, AI-native product studio, software house,
engineering lab, automation partner, fractional product team, training organization, or another
form. Define the smallest paid engagement, the offer ladder, deliverables, duration, price logic,
client responsibilities, acceptance criteria, and transition from project to support or retainer.

### 14. What does “AI proficiency” mean here?

Define it through observable capabilities rather than language. Rank what is genuinely proven today:
AI readiness and strategy, training, workflow redesign, automation, agents, RAG, evaluation,
fine-tuning, vision, speech, forecasting, data engineering, integrations, edge deployment,
observability, security, and production operation. Name the strongest evidence for each major claim.

### 15. Why should anyone choose us over the alternatives?

Compare Joka Source with doing nothing, using off-the-shelf software, hiring freelancers, using an
ordinary agency, engaging a large consultancy, building an internal team, or letting the client use
AI tools directly. Identify the advantage we have now, the advantage we can build, the trade-offs we
accept, and the clients or work we should deliberately reject.

## Portfolio, projects, and credible case studies

### 16. What is the complete project universe?

List every candidate project and classify it as commercial client work, foundation cell, internal
tool, open-source contribution, owned product, research, prototype, experiment, archived work, or
planned concept. Record owner, contributors, dates, users, current status, public links, and whether
it belongs on `.org`, `.com`, both, or neither.

### 17. What was the condition before each important project?

For each candidate case study, describe who had the problem, what they were trying to accomplish,
the workflow and tools they used, delays and failures, emotional and operational friction, prior
attempts, the moment the problem became undeniable, and what was at stake if nothing changed.

### 18. What did Joka Source actually contribute?

State the constraints, alternatives considered, why AI or software was—or was not—appropriate, the
research, strategy, design, engineering, integration, deployment, training, and support performed,
and the exact boundary between Joka Source’s contribution and work done by clients, employers,
partners, students, or other teams.

### 19. What changed, and what proves it?

Separate shipped outputs, measured outcomes, observed qualitative changes, client statements,
founder beliefs, and planned benefits. For every number, provide baseline, measurement window, data
source, sample, attribution limits, and dated evidence. Include what failed, changed late,
underperformed, or taught us something important.

### 20. What may be published about each project?

Record permission to use the project and client names, logos, quotations, screenshots, metrics,
architecture, code, and public status. Identify NDA, privacy, security, dignity, IP, employer, and
institutional constraints; decide how anonymous work remains specific without being identifiable;
and label live work, pilots, prototypes, research, and plans unmistakably.

### 21. How should the portfolio create credibility?

Choose whether to organize it by industry, problem, capability, engagement type, audience, or
status. Define which projects deserve deep case studies, short cards, research notes, open-source
pages, future-concept pages, or no page; the minimum evidence for inclusion; and the condition-to-
case-study chain used throughout: **Condition → Stakes → Constraints → Intervention → Outcome →
Evidence → Learning → Relevance**.

## Positioning, story, identity, and experience

### 22. What single promise can Joka Source credibly own?

Write separate but related positioning statements for `.org` and `.com`: audience, urgent problem,
category, distinctive mechanism, benefit, alternatives, reason to believe, and deliberate sacrifice.
Identify what is true now, what is a commitment, and what would be overclaiming.

### 23. What story should people remember and repeat?

Define the human protagonist, the opposing pattern rather than villain, what changes, Joka Source’s
role, the emotional tension, the origin story, and the future invitation. Decide the role of “Built
to outlive us,” “Software should survive graduation,” “Autonomy without amnesia,” and any commercial
counterpart.

### 24. How should the brand architecture and naming work?

Decide whether Joka Source is the master brand, an endorsing trust mark, or one brand among mostly
independent products. Define how foundation cells, commercial projects, owned products, research,
and retired work inherit names, identity, standards, reputation, and credit; and whether “Foundation,”
“Source,” “cell,” “CRISPR,” and “Joka” should be public or internal language.

### 25. How should Joka Source sound?

Define the voice through behaviors and examples: precise or expressive, institutional or insurgent,
technical or human, prestigious or accessible, warm or sober. Specify how voice changes across
student recruitment, consulting sales, case studies, technical writing, governance debate, privacy
incidents, project retirement, and founder speech; and list prohibited hype, affiliation claims,
jargon, and phrases.

### 26. How should Joka Source look and feel?

Define the desired first-three-second feeling and thirty-second understanding, the visual spectrums
the brand should occupy, authentic source material from Joka or project work, what may be shared or
distinct across the two domains, and what would look misleading, official, elitist, childish,
generic, fashionable, or copied. Include accessibility, mobile, low-bandwidth, print, and dark-mode
requirements.

### 27. What journeys should the two websites create?

For `.org` and `.com`, map entry → understanding → trust → proof → primary action → follow-up →
repeat relationship. Decide the homepage hierarchy, required sections, portfolio depth, product and
research separation, CTA, form data, response owner and service level, cross-domain navigation,
content-management ownership, and safeguards against stale claims.

## Activation, measurement, risk, and execution

### 28. How will people discover, trust, and act on the brand?

Choose the first 12-month behavior for each front, the channels where genuine attention already
exists, the proof-led content only Joka Source can publish, partner and founder roles, onboarding and
sales capacity behind every CTA, launch trigger, sustainable publishing cadence, and what should
never become content.

### 29. How will we know the strategy is working?

Set 30-day, 90-day, 12-month, and post-founder outcomes with baselines, targets, dates, and owners.
Include leading indicators, repeat behavior, commercial pipeline and delivery measures, qualitative
evidence, vanity metrics to ignore, review cadence, and the evidence that would disprove the
positioning or force a change.

### 30. What must be decided, resourced, protected, and owned before launch?

Name the final approver, vetoes, evidence that can overrule taste, founder hours, team and agency
roles, budget, legal and institutional review, security and privacy gates, fixed dates, dependencies,
top trust failures, incident decision-maker, response process, maintenance owner, and the precise
definition of “done” for strategy, identity, website, portfolio, launch, and handover.

## Closing synthesis

After both founders answer, compare responses and record:

1. Agreed facts and decisions.
2. Unproven beliefs requiring research.
3. Material founder disagreements.
4. Legal, institutional, commercial, or ethical blockers.
5. Claims that already have evidence and permission.
6. The five decisions that must be made before naming, identity, copy, or website production begins.
