# JSF Brand Strategic Hypotheses — Desk Discovery v0

> Agency point of view before founder and stakeholder interviews. These are not approved strategy.
> Each item is marked as a known fact, provisional inference, contradiction, risk, or opportunity so
> the existing material does not quietly become truth by repetition.

## Executive diagnosis

JSF’s strongest idea is not “open-source software for IIM Calcutta.” That describes the output and
the community, but many people can make software and publish code. The more ownable idea is that JSF
designs continuity into student-built institutions: ownership can pass, authority can be earned,
decisions remain inspectable, and projects can retire without erasing what was learned.

The present brand has a compelling founding line—“built to outlive us”—and an unusually rich
operating metaphor, but its public meaning is ahead of its proof and sometimes ahead of its legal or
institutional clarity. The next step is not to make the existing identity more polished. It is to
decide what JSF is allowed to claim, who must believe it first, which behavior matters now, and how
the foundation creates value beyond the individual products.

## Known facts from current canonical material

1. **Foundation mission.** The draft constitution states that JSF exists to build software for the
   IIM Calcutta community that outlives the people who build it.
2. **Founding model.** JSF adapts a CRISPR cell lifecycle previously used at IIIT Nagpur: small,
   autonomous cells spawn, operate, hand over, or retire with knowledge preserved.
3. **Governance premise.** The draft constitution favors continuity over authorship, openness by
   default, contribution-gated authority, cell autonomy, designed retirement, and service to the
   campus community.
4. **Current cells.** Current foundation material identifies Joka CV, AlumERP, and Joka Connect as
   incubating/forming cells.
5. **Founding status.** The constitution is draft v0 and not yet ratified. Several governing,
   licensing, legal, ownership, contribution-threshold, funding, and retirement decisions remain
   open.
6. **Brand status.** The canonical foundation brand palette, typography, logo rules, and foundational
   voice document are still placeholders.
7. **Provisional public identity.** The website intentionally uses a provisional warm-paper,
   black-ink, signal-coral, acid-green, editorial system because the permanent brand is not defined.
8. **Existing public narrative.** The website leads with “Software should survive graduation,”
   “Autonomy without amnesia,” and “Your first contribution is your application.”
9. **Existing social posture.** The social guide prefers alumni-rooted, sober, open-source-first,
   credit-first communication and makes ongoing maintenance and trade-offs part of the content.
10. **Primary current conversion.** The foundation site directs people to inspect project cells,
    read the constitution, and contribute through GitHub.

## High-priority contradictions to resolve

### 1. Institution, community, or independent initiative

- The constitution describes JSF as rooted in IIM Calcutta and serving its community.
- The social voice says “alumni-rooted, not institution-rooted” and warns against sounding official.
- The website says the software “belongs to the institution.”
- The entity-status document is unresolved.

**Why it matters:** these statements imply different ownership, endorsement, accountability, and
permission. The right positioning, name use, disclaimer, visual codes, partnerships, and product
trust cannot be decided until the factual relationship is explicit.

### 2. Foundation, product umbrella, or contributor institution

The current story gives roughly equal weight to governance model, project portfolio, and contributor
invitation. It does not yet declare which role is primary.

**Why it matters:** a product user, contributor, alum, recruiter, and administrator enter for
different reasons. Without a hierarchy, the homepage and campaigns may make everyone understand a
little but nobody act strongly.

### 3. Open invitation versus developer-shaped doorway

The constitution welcomes meaningful issues and contributions, but the current primary action is a
GitHub contribution. The language and mechanics privilege people already comfortable with public
repositories.

**Why it matters:** design, research, writing, operations, mentorship, institutional knowledge, and
community work may be essential to continuity. If they lack legitimate paths, “open” becomes
technically true but experientially narrow.

### 4. Continuity promise versus founder-period reality

“Built to outlive us” is memorable. Yet the foundation was founded in July 2026, the constitution is
unratified, current cells lack several owners/thresholds/retirement conditions, and the visible
system has not yet demonstrated a post-founder handover.

**Why it matters:** the line should operate as a design commitment backed by mechanisms and measured
milestones, not as a completed achievement.

### 5. Cell metaphor versus audience comprehension

The cell model is strategically rich internally. “CRISPR” also carries strong gene-editing and
biotechnology associations, while “cell” can imply biology, prisons, politics, terrorism, or small
organizational units depending on context.

**Why it matters:** JSF must choose whether the metaphor is the public category story, a secondary
explanation, or internal governance language. Teaching cost and unintended associations need testing.

### 6. Products versus master brand

Joka CV, AlumERP, and Joka Connect have different users, risk profiles, maturity, and likely product
identities. Current material does not decide how visibly they inherit or endorse JSF.

**Why it matters:** uncontrolled independence wastes foundation equity; over-centralization can make
the products harder to understand and transfer risk between them.

## Provisional strategic hypotheses to test

### H1 — The central problem is institutional amnesia, not software scarcity

Campus communities do not mainly lack ideas or code. They lose context, ownership, trust, and
maintainers as cohorts turn over. “Autonomy without amnesia” may express the operating solution more
distinctively than “open source.”

**Test:** ask students/alumni for recent examples of useful work dying and what actually caused the
failure. If continuity is not a felt problem, identify the nearer pain that makes it relevant.

### H2 — JSF should be an endorsing system of trust, not the product people must use

Most users will seek a CV, alumni, or connection outcome—not foundation governance. JSF may create
the most value as the visible guarantee behind cells: community-rooted stewardship, inspectable
decisions, contribution paths, privacy boundaries, and succession discipline.

**Test:** compare comprehension, trust, and action between master-brand-led and product-led messages.

### H3 — “Built to outlive us” is the strongest current line if presented as a commitment

The line is human, memorable, founder-accountable, and extends beyond software. Its credibility
depends on visible proof and careful tense.

**Test:** measure unaided interpretation. Check whether audiences hear continuity, mortality,
founder ego, or a generic legacy claim.

### H4 — The next cohort is the emotional protagonist

The founders start the story, but the person who inherits and improves the work embodies the brand’s
promise. This moves JSF away from founder mythology and gives current contributors a meaningful role.

**Test:** compare founder-origin-led, current-user-led, and next-cohort-led narratives for emotional
relevance and willingness to participate.

### H5 — Handover should become a public ritual and primary proof asset

JSF can make continuity tangible through cell-health records, handover releases, contributor
lineage, decision trails, succession milestones, and retirement retrospectives.

**Test:** determine which artifacts create trust for contributors, alumni, and institutional
stakeholders without producing documentation theatre.

### H6 — The brand should feel like a living public record

The current editorial website direction has a credible strategic seed: paper, ink, marginalia,
records, and high-signal color can bridge archival continuity with active building. It should evolve
from a fashionable editorial aesthetic into a system rooted in the actual artifacts of contribution
and handover.

**Test:** compare this territory with alternatives rooted in campus culture, open-source mechanics,
and cell/lifecycle behavior. Reject any route that looks official without permission or like a
generic developer brand.

### H7 — “Contribution” must be redesigned as multiple legitimate actions

A contribution system that names code, design, research, documentation, mentoring, data stewardship,
operations, and institutional knowledge can expand participation while preserving merit through
observable work.

**Test:** prototype role-based first actions and measure completion, response burden, and perceived
legitimacy among technical and non-technical participants.

### H8 — Foundation marketing should be proof-led, not announcement-led

Build logs, public decisions, contributor journeys, handovers, and responsible retirement are not
supporting content; they are evidence of the brand. A major launch without such proof would make the
story feel aspirational and founder-centric.

**Test:** identify which existing operational artifacts generate meaningful trust and qualified
action, then make one recurring proof format sustainable.

## Recommended strategic posture before discovery

Until the founders resolve the contradictions and research tests the hypotheses:

- Treat all current identity choices as provisional.
- Keep “built to outlive us” as a promising line, not a ratified claim of achieved continuity.
- Do not imply official IIM Calcutta affiliation, institutional ownership, or endorsement.
- Describe current legal/governance status precisely rather than borrowing authority from the word
  “foundation.”
- Lead any activation with a real working proof and an operationally supported next action.
- Do not expand channel cadence until contributor/product response capacity is owned.
- Preserve the sober, credit-first, transparent tone already present in the social guide.
- Avoid making CRISPR the dominant public mnemonic until comprehension testing is complete.

## Preliminary strategy frame

This is a stimulus for discovery, not final positioning:

> **For people who want useful work at IIM Calcutta to survive the batch that began it, Joka Source
> Foundation is the open stewardship system behind community-built projects. Unlike one-off student
> teams or founder-owned products, JSF makes contribution, decisions, succession, and even retirement
> inspectable—so each cohort can inherit working software and make it stronger.**

### What this frame deliberately sacrifices

- It does not position JSF as an official IIM Calcutta body.
- It does not promise that every current project has already survived a handover.
- It makes the foundation’s value stewardship and continuity, not generic innovation.
- It allows products to lead with user value while earning trust through a JSF endorsement.

## First behavior objective to debate

The recommended first brand objective is not broad awareness. It is to create a small, measurable
pipeline of qualified non-founder participants who complete a first meaningful contribution and then
return for a second.

Why this matters:

- It builds the actual continuity capability behind the promise.
- It produces proof, stories, leaders, and distribution.
- It tests whether the contribution system works beyond founder proximity.
- It is more strategically valuable than followers or launch impressions.

The founders must decide the 12-month numeric target, eligible contribution types, response standard,
and product/cell focus during discovery.

## Decisions required before creative development

1. Factual/legal relationship with IIM Calcutta and mandatory disclosure.
2. Meaning of “foundation” today and intended entity path.
3. Primary audience and primary 12-month behavior.
4. Foundation–cell brand architecture.
5. The credible master promise and current proof.
6. Whether CRISPR/cell is public or internal language.
7. Geographic/institutional scope over five years.
8. Contribution paths and who may earn authority.
9. Accountable brand approver and feedback protocol.
10. Real launch capacity, budget, date, and response ownership.

## External lenses used

The process intentionally combines a human/community view of brand strategy with an execution and
operational view. Anneli Hansson’s public framing emphasizes brand as shared mission, culture,
identity, community, meaning, and momentum—not simply identity design. Friso Hylkema’s public
positioning emphasizes the processes, governance, accountability, and metrics needed for reliable
scaling. Those are useful lenses for JSF because its promise lives or dies through both felt
belonging and operational continuity. They are lenses, not authorities on JSF’s answer.
